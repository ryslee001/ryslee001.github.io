Car Picker Helper: comparing cars without the noise

A Flask web app for car buyers who've narrowed it down to a few options and want a clear, objective comparison.

RoleDesigner & developer
TeamSolo
StackPython, Flask, requests
DataNHTSA, EPA fueleconomy.gov

Why I built it

I've loved cars since I was a kid, from going to the Seattle International Auto Show with my dad every year to now owning one myself. When my dad started shopping for a new car, I saw how exhausting the research was: subjective reviews, dense spec sheets and forums full of conflicting opinions.

Buying a car is one of the biggest financial decisions most people make. I wanted to build something that lets buyers compare a shortlist on the things that matter, using data no one is trying to sell them.

How it works

The app pulls data from three free government APIs and turns it into two 0–100 scores per vehicle, each shown as a bar that goes from red to amber to green.

EPAFuel EconomyThe official combined MPG rating, scaled from 10 MPG (heavy truck) to 55 MPG (hybrid)
NHTSAComplaintsSafety complaints filed by owners. Every 5 complaints subtracts 1 point. Counts for 60% of the Reliability score
NHTSARecallsConfirmed defects. Each recall subtracts 10 points. Counts for 40% of the Reliability score
reliability = 0.6 × complaint_score + 0.4 × recall_score fuel_score = normalize(comb08_mpg, 10, 55) → 0–100

Each API has its own Flask route that makes the request on the server and returns processed JSON to the frontend. That keeps the browser from blocking direct calls to the government APIs (a CORS restriction).

Design decisions

  • A recall is a confirmed defect, while a complaint is an owner's report that no one has verified. Complaints matter in large numbers, so they get a gentler penalty that adds up.
  • Other tools I looked at, including a multi-API VIN decoder and an NHTSA chat assistant, show raw records. Mine reduces everything to two numbers you can compare side by side.
  • The fuel economy API needs a vehicle-ID lookup before it returns full data. If no variant matches, the app handles it gracefully instead of crashing.

What I cut, and why

I originally planned to compare horsepower and torque, but the only API with good coverage was behind a paywall, and the free ones didn't cover enough vehicles. I dropped that comparison and stuck with government data that covers more model years.

I also dropped trim-level comparison, because the APIs don't distinguish trims that share an engine. Cutting scope to what the data could reliably support made for a more honest tool.

Known limitations

Raw complaint counts favor newer cars. A 2007 Camry has had far more years of ownership, and far more owners, than a 2022 model. So it will have more complaints on file even if it isn't less reliable. The next version should adjust complaint counts for sales volume and vehicle age.
